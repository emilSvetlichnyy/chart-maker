Before you start

What can change whether a bar chart is the right choice.

Use bars when the question is “which is bigger?” across months, regions, or products.
Start the value axis at zero when you can — truncated axes exaggerate differences.
If categories are parts of one total, a pie chart may tell the story faster.

Build a practical comparison

Bar charts shine when readers scan for highs and lows. Keep the set focused, label clearly, and export when the ranking is obvious — homework, standup updates, and quick reports included.

Same units everywhere

Don’t mix currencies, percentages, and counts on one axis without saying so.

Short category names

Long labels wrap badly on phones. Abbreviate, then explain in a caption if needed.

Highlight the takeaway

If one bar is the point, say it in the title: “April led sales” beats “Chart 1”.

When should I use a bar chart instead of a pie?

Use a bar chart to compare amounts. Use a pie when you care about shares of one whole.
